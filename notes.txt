Given your new temporal transport basis, I think the spatial component should become a Lie-consistent local twist transport.
The key observation is that your temporal basis outputs
\[
u_i(t)=
[v_i(t),\omega_i(t),\eta_i(t)],
\]
so you already have something physically meaningful: translational velocity, angular velocity, and scale rate. Instead of fitting a generic cubic polynomial through these values, use the geometry of rigid motion directly.
For a neighbor \(j\), its motion can be transported to Gaussian \(i\) as
\[
\boxed{
v_{j\rightarrow i}
=
v_j+
\omega_j\times(x_i-x_j)
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\omega_{j\rightarrow i}=\omega_j
}
\]
and, initially,
\[
\eta_{j\rightarrow i}=\eta_j.
\]
This formula is important. If several Gaussians belong to the same rigid part, their translational velocities are not supposed to be equal. A rotating object naturally has
\[
v(x)=v_0+\omega\times(x-x_0).
\]
Cubic MLS does not explicitly know this. A Lie/twist transport does.
So if an arm rotates, a Gaussian farther from the joint should move faster automatically. You don't need a cubic spatial polynomial to discover that relation from data.
I would keep KNN, but use it only to find candidate neighbors. Then each neighbor \(j\) proposes a transported motion at \(i\):
\[
u_{j\rightarrow i}(t)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
v_j+\omega_j\times(x_i-x_j)\\
\omega_j\\
\eta_j
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Aggregate them:
\[
\boxed{
u_i^{local}
=
\frac{
\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}
w_{ij}u_{j\rightarrow i}
}{
\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}w_{ij}
}
}
\]
but make \(w_{ij}\) motion-aware, not just distance-based:
\[
w_{ij}
=
w^{geo}_{ij}
w^{motion}_{ij}.
\]
For example,
\[
w^{geo}_{ij}
=
\exp
\left(
-\frac{\|x_i-x_j\|^2}{2\sigma_x^2}
\right)
\]
and because your temporal dictionary gives every Gaussian coefficients \(a_i\),
\[
w^{motion}_{ij}
=
\exp
\left(
-\frac{\|a_i-a_j\|^2}{2\sigma_a^2}
\right).
\]
This is exactly where your new temporal representation and spatial representation complement each other.
Two Gaussians can be physically close but have
\[
a_i\neq a_j.
\]
Then their edge becomes weak.
